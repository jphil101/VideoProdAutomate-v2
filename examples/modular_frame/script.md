Modular steel frames grow by repeating one structural bay, six metres wide and seven and a half metres deep.
The first bay is already framed and decked, so the tower crane picks up the next primary beam from the laydown yard.
The hoist lifts it clear, and the crane slews it over the frame while the load's sway is cancelled out.
It is lowered between columns B and C on grid line one, and settles onto the column caps under its own weight.
Then the crane returns to the yard for its twin, and flies it across to the back of the frame.
It is set on grid line two, exactly parallel, so the new bay matches the first.
The connections are then bolted, locking each beam to its columns.
Next, a precast hollow-core floor unit is rigged at four points and flown in.
It drops onto the beams and completes the second module.
Every bay is identical, so the frame can keep extending along the grid as the building grows.
